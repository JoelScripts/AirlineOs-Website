SEP 27, 2026 · DEVLOG 003

First live flight

Every proof so far has been run with the sim muted, the flight scripted, or both. Today we took BA1470 — London Heathrow to Glasgow out of the gate against a live MSFS session with a real SimBrief flight plan loaded, and let the cabin talk. The live chain held end to end, and the run earned its keep by finding two bugs that no amount of muted testing could have surfaced.

WHAT WENT GREEN

  • The live chain, end to end. Flight-plan identity resolved, the flight-phase engine tracked the aircraft through the phases, the trigger router picked the right event, and correctly-worded cabin announcements fired over the sim. Each of those links had been proven on its own; this is the first time they've all run together against a real flight.
  • The wording was right. What the passengers heard came from the templates as designed — airline identity, flight number and route in the right places — not from a demo script.
  • A local neural voice cast was ear-approved the same day — British cabin crew and US dispatch voices, fully offline — so the lines now have a voice worth listening to.

WHAT IT CAUGHT (THE HONEST PART)

  • A silent-voice bug. Some announcement lines rendered and then never played: a playback tail was orphaned after an early return in the speaker path, so the audio was built and dropped. A muted test can't catch this — by definition nothing is supposed to be heard — only a live run did. It's now guarded by an automated regression test.
  • Announcement spam. GSX oscillated between its pushback and taxi states, so a once-per-phase announcement re-fired three times in ninety seconds. Fixed with a 120-second refractory gate on each trigger — the state has to stay settled before the line can fire again — and that gate is regression-tested too.

WHAT IT MEANS

We cancelled the run at taxi, read the logs, fixed what they showed, and re-verified. That's the normal build loop — the only difference is that this time it happened on a real flight with everything switched on. Nothing here is a design problem; both bugs were small, local, and closed the same day.

Next: a full, uncancelled gate-to-gate run — pushback to arrival, no log review in the middle of it. Dates are unchanged: closed alpha Dec 2026 – Jan 2027, Cabin v1.0 estimated March 2027.
