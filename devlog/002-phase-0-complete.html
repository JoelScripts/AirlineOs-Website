SEP 26, 2026 · DEVLOG 002

Phase 0 complete — the sim answers back

Devlog 001 closed with "the two riskiest unknowns are retired." That was this morning's position. It's now evening, and Phase 0 — prove that Airline OS can actually be built — is closed. Every feasibility question has been asked, answered, and written down. Here's the honest receipt.

WHAT WE PROVED

  • The sim hands over your identity, live. Swap a livery mid-session and the registration and flight number follow instantly. Meanwhile the sim's own airline field comes back empty — which is exactly the gap the resolution chain exists to fill.
  • The flow works both ways. An external process wrote a callsign into the sim's ATC identity (INI001 → OS100), it held across sessions, and a second, independent process watched it happen. That's the "native ATC sync" feature, proven end to end.
  • A real flight plan feeds real announcements. A genuine SimBrief OFP was fetched — sixteen data points (callsign, route, fuel, payload, weather at both ends) now have verified paths into the template engine.
  • The first trigger fired. We switched the beacon from outside the sim; the event watcher saw it hold, debounced it, and fired the mapped line: "beacon on — flight attendants, prepare for departure." Edge → debounce → trigger → announcement. That's the cabin's nervous system, working.

WHAT BROKE (THE HONEST PART)

  • Remote door movement was refused. We tried to close a door from outside; the sim said no. Fine — Airline OS only ever reads door state. Finding, not failure.
  • Two "writable" flags in our tooling were wrong — in opposite directions. One var claimed writable and wasn't; another claimed read-only and was. New house rule: every variable gets tested empirically before we trust it.
  • Our automated full-flight test caught a watcher bug — a door left open at startup would never announce closing. It surfaced in testing, before a single user could have hit it. The fix shipped the same day.

WHAT'S NEXT

Building. The flight-phase engine already passes a complete gate-to-gate simulation — all fourteen phases, from boarding to arrival, with jump protection when the sim fast-forwards. Next come the announcement templates (user-editable plain text, by design), then the voice pipeline, then the first full Cabin run against a live flight.

Dates are unchanged: closed alpha Dec 2026 – Jan 2027, Cabin v1.0 estimated March 2027. Phase 1 is open.
