Introducing Airline OS

Every serious sim pilot eventually builds the same imaginary thing: an airline. You pick a livery, you memorise a callsign, you file the same rotation in SimBrief. But when you're actually in the seat, the sim doesn't know any of it. The cabin is silent, the ground crew doesn't care what you filed, and native ATC calls you "heavy" instead of your airline.

Airline OS is the missing layer. It's an external companion app for MSFS 2024 that turns those disconnected moments into one operation — and it ships as three modules:

  • CABIN — crew, passengers and spoken announcements from gate to gate, using your airline's name and your flight number.
  • DISPATCH — company messages in the cruise, straight from your SimBrief OFP, signed by The Company.
  • RAMP — ground services choreographed around the flight you actually filed.

NO AIRLINE PACKS. EVER.

The reason this doesn't exist yet is that everyone solves it with content packs: buy the airline, get the announcements. We think that's backwards. Your identity already exists — in your livery, in your SimBrief flight plan, in the sim itself. Airline OS reads it live: manual override → SimBrief OFP → SimConnect live values → generic fallback. Configure nothing and it still works. That's the whole point.

WHERE IT STANDS

Project status today: the two riskiest unknowns are already retired. SimConnect identity values are confirmed readable (Spike 1), and the SimBrief OFP fetch works without an API key (Spike 2). The first-run onboarding flow — which asks you for your SimBrief Pilot ID instead of hardcoding anything — is specced. Next up is the Cabin MVP spec: event triggers, announcement templates and the audio pipeline.

The public estimate for the first payware module is March 2027, with Dispatch and Ramp following through the year. The roadmap and timeline on the homepage are the same ones we plan against — when a phase closes, it gets updated here first.

WHY THE DEVLOG

No newsletter, no hype cycle — just build notes. Each post will cover what shipped, what broke, and what changed in the plan. If you want to follow along, the repo and this devlog are the two places that matter.
